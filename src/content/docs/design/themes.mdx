A theme sets the fonts, colors, background, text effects and default layouts for your slides. It also includes options to make text easier to read over the background. Every presentation has one theme. Slides follow it unless you change something on a slide itself.

## Built-in themes

Guidepost includes seven themes: **Clean** (the default for new presentations), **Quiet**, **Statement**, **Reverent**, **Cinematic**, **Grain** and **Linen**. Built-in themes can't be changed or deleted, but you can edit a copy.

## Apply a theme

- **To the whole presentation:** click **Presentation** above the slide list, then click a theme under **Theme** in the inspector.
- **To one group,** such as a song: right-click the group and choose **Apply Theme to Group**, or select one of its slides and click **Change…** beside the theme in the inspector.
- **When adding a song or passage:** choose a theme in the picker's theme strip.
- **From the Library:** select a theme in **Library › Themes** and click **Apply to presentation**, or double-click it on the Library drawer's **Themes** tab.

After a theme change, a notice summarizes what changed, for example "Updated 24 slides · 2 slides keep their own look", with **Undo**.

## Overriding the theme on one slide

You can give a slide its own background, transition or other settings. The inspector marks overridden properties with a small dot and shows **Reset to theme** beside them. Slides with overrides keep those settings when the theme changes. Everything else follows the theme.

## Edit a theme

<Steps>

1. Click **Edit Theme…** under the presentation's theme in the inspector, or right-click a theme thumbnail and choose **Edit Theme…**.

2. Change what you need. The preview on the right shows the theme on a Text, Scripture, Song and Announcement slide as you work.

3. Click **Save Theme**. The line above the buttons tells you how many slides in this presentation will update.

</Steps>

Editing a built-in theme makes your own copy (marked **Built-in · copied**). Type a new name at the top to tell it apart.

### What a theme sets

| Section | Settings |
|---|---|
| **Fonts** | Heading and body font, each with a weight. |
| **Colors** | Text color, accent color, and a **Palette** of colors that appear first in every color picker. |
| **Background** | A color, gradient, image, video or scene, with optional dim. See [Backgrounds](/design/backgrounds/). |
| **Text effect** | **None**, **Soft shadow**, **Strong** shadow or **Outline**. |
| **Legibility** | **None**, **Grain**, **Vignette** or **Scrim**, to keep text readable over busy backgrounds. |
| **Defaults per kind** | The default layout for Text, Scripture and Song slides; **lines per slide** for songs (2 or 4); and the **transition** (Cut or Fade, with a duration). |

## Make a new theme

- **From a slide:** select a slide you've styled and click **Save as Theme…** at the bottom of the inspector. The theme editor opens with that slide's look.
- **From another theme:** open it in the theme editor and click **Duplicate**.
- **From scratch:** in **Library › Themes**, click **New theme**.

## Share themes

Click **Export…** in the theme editor, or **Export** in **Library › Themes**, to save a theme file. Import one with **Import theme…**, under the presentation's themes in the inspector or in **Library › Themes**. Theme files let you use the same look on another Mac or share it with another church.

## Delete a theme

Click **Delete theme…** in the theme editor, or select the theme in **Library › Themes** and click **Delete…**. Slides that used it keep their look.

<Aside type="tip">
A presentation file includes the themes it uses, so it looks the same on another Mac even if that Mac doesn't have your themes.
</Aside>

## Fonts

The font menu has three sections:

- **Recently used**: fonts you’ve used recently.
- **Guidepost fonts**: Inter, Source Serif 4, Playfair Display, Roboto Slab, Caveat and JetBrains Mono. These are built into Guidepost, so they look the same on every Mac and on network viewers.
- **Mac fonts**: every font installed on your Mac, including fonts added with Font Book.

Each name is shown in its own typeface, and you can search by name. If a presentation uses a font that isn't installed on the Mac it's opened on, Guidepost says so once, shows a similar built-in font instead, and keeps your original choice in the file.

## Colors

Color pickers show your theme's palette first, then recently used colors, then presets. You can type a hex value, set the opacity, or use the eyedropper to sample a color from anywhere on screen. **More…** opens the standard macOS color panel.
