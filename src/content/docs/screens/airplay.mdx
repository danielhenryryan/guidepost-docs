A TV with AirPlay built in, or any TV with an Apple TV attached, can be an audience screen without a cable to your Mac. Once connected, it supports holds, video and transitions like a wired audience screen.

## Connect the TV

macOS manages AirPlay connections, so you connect the TV from Control Center. Guidepost can't start the connection itself.

<Steps>

1. Make sure your Mac and the TV are on the same network. A guest network usually won't work.

2. In the menu bar, open **Control Center**, then click **Screen Mirroring**.

3. Choose the TV.

4. Choose **Use as Extended Display**, not **Mirror**.

5. In Guidepost, open the [Screens panel](/screens/screens-panel/) and turn on the TV under **Audience screens**.

</Steps>

Guidepost remembers the TV by name. Next week, connect it from Control Center the same way, and Guidepost restores its role automatically.

## If the TV isn't connected

When a remembered AirPlay TV isn't connected, the Screens panel lists it as **Not connected** with the connection steps, and the line under **GO LIVE** says so:

> Sanctuary TV (AirPlay) not connected · Show me how

Click **Show me how** to see the steps. **Open Displays Settings** opens System Settings › Displays, where you can also check the TV's arrangement. You can see the same steps at any time from **Help › Connecting an AirPlay Display**.

## If the TV is mirroring your screen

If **Mirror** was chosen instead of **Use as Extended Display**, the TV shows a copy of your own screen, controls and all. Guidepost detects this, won't put the slides on it, and shows a banner:

> Sanctuary TV is mirroring your screen. Choose Use as Extended Display in Screen Mirroring.

Click **Show me how** (or **Fix** under **GO LIVE**), then in Control Center › Screen Mirroring, choose **Use as Extended Display** under the TV.

## Video sound

When an AirPlay TV connects, macOS may send your Mac's sound to it. For each AirPlay audience screen, the Screens panel has a **Video sound** choice:

- **This Mac / sound system**: video sound plays through your Mac's sound output, for example into the church's sound desk.
- **The display**: video sound plays on the TV.

The choice is remembered for each TV. It affects only Guidepost's videos, not your Mac's other sound.

## Staying connected

While an AirPlay TV is an audience screen during a service, Guidepost keeps your Mac awake so the connection doesn't drop when the Mac would otherwise sleep.

If the TV drops off the network during a service, the service keeps going on your other screens, and a banner says:

> Sanctuary TV (AirPlay) disconnected. Reconnect it from Screen Mirroring.

Reconnect it from Control Center. Guidepost puts the slides back on it automatically.

<Aside type="tip">
For a more stable connection, connect the TV (or its Apple TV) to the network with an Ethernet cable rather than Wi-Fi.
</Aside>
